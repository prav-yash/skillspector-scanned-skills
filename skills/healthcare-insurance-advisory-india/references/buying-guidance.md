# Buying Guidance — Fact-Based, No Product Endorsements

*Core stance: this skill never declares any insurer or product "the best." It helps the user compare options on verifiable facts and decide for themselves. Recommendations are always framed as "based on the criteria you gave, these facts differentiate the options" — never as an endorsement.*

## Step 1 — Establish the buyer's facts before anything else

Do not search or suggest products until you know (ask in one compact set of questions, not an interrogation):

- Who is being insured (self / spouse / parents / children) and their **ages**
- **Known pre-existing conditions** (diabetes, hypertension, cardiac history, etc.) — this drives waiting-period and co-pay analysis more than anything else
- Rough **sum insured** they're considering and budget comfort
- City/tier (affects hospital costs and thus adequate sum insured)
- Existing coverage (employer group policy? existing individual policy that could be ported or topped up?)
- Structure preference if relevant: **family floater vs. separate individual policies** (for older parents, a separate policy usually deserves consideration — floaters price on the eldest member — but present this as a factual trade-off, not a directive)

## Step 2 — The factual comparison criteria (stable knowledge)

These are the facts that differentiate policies. Compare candidates on these, citing each policy's own brochure/policy wording:

| Criterion | Why it matters | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| PED waiting period | Capped at 36 months by the 2024 Master Circular, but shorter is better | Exact months in policy wording |
| Co-payment | Common in senior-citizen plans; directly reduces every payout | % and whether it's optional/removable |
| Room-rent cap | Triggers proportionate deduction across the whole bill | Cap as ₹ or % of SI; "no cap" plans exist |
| Restoration/refill benefit | Whether SI refills after a claim in the same year | Conditions on same-illness restoration |
| Sub-limits on named procedures | Cataract, knee replacement etc. often capped | The sub-limit schedule |
| Day-care & domiciliary coverage | Many treatments no longer need 24-hr admission | Coverage lists |
| Pre/post-hospitalization window | Diagnostics and follow-ups add up | Days covered before/after |
| No-claim bonus | How SI grows in claim-free years | % and cap |
| Insurer's claim settlement & complaint record | Fact-based signal of claims experience | IRDAI annual report data / insurer's published claim ratios — cite the source and year |
| Network hospitals near the user | Practical cashless access | Insurer's hospital locator for their city |

## Step 3 — Search rules (when finding current options)

- Search only **after** Step 1 facts are known — otherwise results are generic.
- Prefer **insurer official product pages and IRDAI publications** over aggregator/affiliate comparison sites; if an aggregator ranking appears, treat it as a lead to verify, never as a conclusion to repeat.
- Present **2–3 candidate options** side by side on the Step 2 criteria — facts per criterion, with the source of each fact.
- **Never state premium figures as fact** — premiums depend on age, city, and medical history. Say "get a quote from the insurer's site" instead of quoting a number from a search snippet.

## Step 4 — How to phrase the recommendation

- ✅ "Based on your parents' ages and your father's diabetes, the facts that matter most are PED waiting period and co-pay. Of the options we compared: Option A has a 24-month PED wait and no mandatory co-pay per its policy wording; Option B has 36 months and 20% co-pay. That difference is the decision point."
- ❌ "Option A is the best health insurance for parents." / "I recommend you buy X."
- Always close with: the policy wording and the insurer's underwriting decision (medical tests, loadings, exclusions offered) are what finally matter — advise reading the policy wording before paying, and using the 30-day free-look period after.
- If the user pushes for a single verdict ("just tell me which one"), explain briefly why a categorical "best" would be dishonest here (it depends on underwriting outcomes and their priorities), then give your comparative facts and clearly name which option the *facts they prioritized* point toward — factual pointing is fine; brand endorsement is not.
