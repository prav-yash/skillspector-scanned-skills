# Claims & IRDAI Mandates

*Last verified: August 2026, based on IRDAI's Master Circular on Health Insurance (May 2024) and Master Circular on Protection of Policyholders' Interests (Sept 2024). These consolidated ~55 older circulars. If the user's situation involves a very recent event, search for anything more recent before relying solely on this file.*

## The two ways a claim gets paid

**Cashless:** The hospital bills the insurer (or its TPA) directly. The user pays only for items not covered by the policy (e.g., non-medical/consumable charges). This is the default IRDAI wants insurers to push toward.

**Reimbursement:** The user pays the hospital upfront, then submits bills/documents to the insurer afterward and gets repaid. This is meant to be the exception now, not the norm — but is still common with non-network hospitals or when pre-authorization wasn't sought.

### Key IRDAI procedural mandates (explain these to users so they know what they're entitled to)

- **Cashless at any hospital, not just network hospitals.** Insurers must offer cashless treatment even at hospitals outside their network, under the "Cashless Everywhere" initiative — the user can request it; the hospital settles directly with the insurer for the covered amount.
- **Response time for pre-authorization:** insurers must decide on a cashless request within **1 hour** for planned/scheduled procedures.
- **Discharge authorization:** insurers must give final cashless authorization within **3 hours** of receiving the hospital's discharge request. If the insurer delays beyond this and the hospital charges extra for the delay, that extra cost is the insurer's responsibility, not the patient's — the patient should not be made to wait in the hospital for this reason.
- **No blanket denial of cashless just because a hospital is "non-network."** Insurers are expected to process cashless requests wherever the user is admitted, subject to policy terms.
- **Free-look period:** for a new policy, the user typically has a window (commonly 15–30 days, varies by policy — don't state a specific number with confidence, point them to their policy schedule) to review and cancel if they don't want it.
- **Portability:** a user can switch insurers without losing continuity benefits (e.g., waiting periods already served, no-claim benefits) — insurers cannot make a portability applicant start their waiting periods from zero, provided the new policy's sum insured isn't higher than the old one for the ported portion.
- **Moratorium on non-disclosure denials:** per the 2024 Master Circular (IRDAI/HLT/CIR/MISC/77/05/2024), after **60 months of continuous coverage** (including ported years), the insurer cannot deny a claim on grounds of non-disclosure or misrepresentation **except in cases of proven fraud** (permanent exclusions still apply). This was reduced from the earlier 8 years. Additionally, PED waiting periods are now capped at **36 months**. Verify against the current circular via `official-sources.md` if the user's dispute hinges on this. This is a significant protection for long-term policyholders — explain it if relevant, but don't promise it applies to their exact policy without them checking.

## What documents are typically needed

For reimbursement claims, insurers commonly ask for:
- Filled claim form
- Original discharge summary
- Original hospital bills + payment receipts
- Investigation reports (lab, imaging) related to the treatment
- ID proof and policy/e-card details
- KYC documents (for larger claims, per AML norms)
- FIR/MLC copy, if the hospitalization was accident-related

Tell users to keep **originals** for insurer submission and their own scanned copies — don't guess whether a *specific* insurer needs something beyond this list; point them to the insurer's claim-intimation page or customer care for the exact checklist.

## How to help a user in the moment

- **About to be hospitalized (planned):** explain pre-authorization — intimate the insurer/TPA before or at admission, ideally 3–4 days ahead for planned procedures if possible, and confirm the hospital has raised the cashless request.
- **Already hospitalized, cashless was denied at the counter:** explain they can still ask the hospital to raise it again citing "Cashless Everywhere," or pay and file for reimbursement afterward — cashless denial doesn't mean the claim itself is invalid.
- **Just got a claim settled short of what was billed:** this is a *partial* settlement, not a rejection — see `rejections-and-grievance.md` for how to seek an explanation and challenge specific deductions.
